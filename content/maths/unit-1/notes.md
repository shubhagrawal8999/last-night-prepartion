## Pass-Fast Summary

- A derivative tells you the **instantaneous rate of change** or the slope of a curve.
- For a power, use the **power rule**: bring the exponent down, then reduce it by one.
- Constants differentiate to **zero**. A constant multiplier stays in front.
- If you have time, practise the chain rule and check your answer by differentiating it again mentally.

## Revision Notes

### The basic idea

Differentiation measures how quickly one quantity changes as another changes. For a graph of $y=f(x)$, the derivative $f'(x)$ gives the slope at a point.

### Rules to remember

- **Power rule:** if $f(x)=x^n$, then $f'(x)=nx^{n-1}$.
- **Constant multiple:** $(cf(x))'=cf'(x)$.
- **Sum rule:** $(f(x)+g(x))'=f'(x)+g'(x)$.
- **Constant rule:** $(c)'=0$.

### Tiny sanity check

The derivative of $x^2$ is $2x$. At $x=3$, the slope is $6$. The slope changes with the point, which is why the answer is a function and not just one number.
