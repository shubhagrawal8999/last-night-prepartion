# Solved Examples

## Example 1: a power

Differentiate $y=4x^3-2x+7$.

1. Apply the power rule to each term: $4(3x^2)-2(1)+0$.
2. Simplify: **$\frac{dy}{dx}=12x^2-2$**.
3. Check: the constant $7$ disappears, and the degree-three term becomes degree two.

## Example 2: a product

Differentiate $y=x^2\sin x$.

1. Set $u=x^2$ and $v=\sin x$.
2. Use $(uv)'=u'v+uv'$.
3. Substitute: $y'=2x\sin x+x^2\cos x$.

Show the rule before substituting. That gives your working a clear trail of marks.
