# Exam Tips

- Always write the phase condition first: in phase means constructive, out of phase means destructive.
- For thin-film questions, never forget the extra $\lambda/2$ phase change due to reflection at a denser medium.
- Use the exact sign convention consistently: maxima and minima conditions are often mixed in examination scripts.
- In ARC questions, remember the minimum thickness corresponds to $m=0$.
- For polarization questions, identify whether the problem is asking about intensity or amplitude before applying Malus's law.
- If a fringe problem gives an air wedge, use $\mu=1$ and remember $\beta = \lambda/(2\alpha)$ for small angles.
- Draw simple ray diagrams when the statement mentions reflection at top and bottom surfaces; this helps catch the correct optical path difference.
- In writing answers, mention that polarization means the electric field oscillates in one plane only.
